Three Axes of Science

Scientists past and present, placed by mathematical sophistication, experimental technique and physical intuition (a feel for how nature behaves, as distinct from mathematical intuition). Scores (0–10) are AI-generated starting points (see the disclaimer below): click anyone to adjust them.

Inspiration. This page grew out of Figure 1 of Daniel Green’s essay Shut Up and Compute: Thoughts on Research in the Era of AI (November 2026). There, physics problem-solving lives in a three-axis space — mathematical sophistication, physical intuition and experimental technique — in which individual researchers occupy uneven regions and AI is one more region whose shape and extent are still unknown. Green’s point is that this unevenness is what leaves room for human science. His figure is schematic; this one places real scientists in that space, as an invitation to argue about who sits where, and where AI might fit.

Disclaimer. All scores, notes and breadth rankings on this page were generated by an AI model (Claude, by Anthropic). They are rough, subjective placements meant only to start a discussion, not measurements or verdicts on anyone’s work, and they do not represent the opinions of the site author in any way. The breadth score in particular is just a formula applied to these three numbers: it does not measure or rank anyone’s scientific impact, importance or achievement in any way.

Shape and colour both encode field. Wall projections in 3D show each point's shadow on the three back planes. The Breadth ranking view orders everyone by this composite, with each person’s individual axis scores marked on their row, so placements that look out of order are easy to spot and fix. “Jitter ties” nudges points with identical integer scores apart (hover shows the true values).
