Rabi oscillations and the double well

Two problems where the exact answer can be compared with an approximation. The first tab drives a spin with a rotating field and compares the Rabi formula with first-order time-dependent perturbation theory. The second tab diagonalizes the quartic double well numerically and compares it with the variational tight-binding ansatz built from two harmonic-oscillator ground states.

Rotating frame. In the frame that rotates with the drive, the Hamiltonian is static, Hrot = −½ħΔσz + εσx = ½ħΩR n·σ. The Bloch vector starts at the south pole |g⟩ and precesses about n at the frequency ΩR. On resonance n lies along x and the spin reaches |e⟩.

Perturbation theory. P(1) = (Ω/Δ)² sin²(Δt/2) grows as (Ωt/2)² on resonance and exceeds 1. Far from resonance it has the right amplitude but the wrong frequency, |Δ| instead of ΩR, so it drifts out of phase after a time of order |Δ|/Ω².

Linear drive. An oscillating field is a co-rotating plus a counter-rotating field. When Ω is not small compared with ω0, the counter-rotating part adds fast wiggles and shifts the resonance (Bloch–Siegert shift): the rotating-wave approximation fails.

Tight binding. The ansatz αLψL + αRψR gives the even ground state ψ+ and the odd state ψ−. Both energies are upper bounds for the exact ones, and both approach ħω/2 as η grows.

Splitting. The tight-binding splitting falls as ηe−η, the exact one as e−2η/3. The Gaussian tails of ψL,R decay too fast under the barrier; the log view shows where.

Doublets. Each level of a single well becomes an even–odd pair. Higher pairs sit closer to the top of the barrier, see a thinner barrier, and are split more. Above the barrier the levels no longer pair up.
