Packet. The packet is the superposition ∫dk c(k) ψk(x) e−iEt/ħ of exact scattering states, Eq. (3.7) of the notes. Its energy distribution |c|² is the shaded curve in the spectrum: a long packet is sharp in energy, a short one is broad.

Resonance. On a resonance the wave between the barriers builds up to |ψ|² ≈ 4/τ1, and the reflected wave on the left vanishes: no standing-wave ripple in the dashed curve. The particle then stays between the barriers for a time of order ħ/Γ before leaking out on both sides.

Short packet. A packet shorter than the well bounces back and forth, and a small pulse leaks out at every hit. Its energy spread covers several resonances, so the transmitted fraction is close to the energy average of τ, not to τ(E0).

Born. In first order, f depends only on the momentum transfer ħq = 2k sin(θ/2), and is the same for g and −g. The exact result is not: compare the strong repulsive and attractive presets.

Forward peak. For k ≪ ħμ only the s-wave is scattered and the cross section is flat in θ. For k ≫ ħμ it is confined to θ ≲ ħμ/k, and at larger angles it follows the Rutherford curve.

Validity. Born works when |g| ≪ 1 at all energies, and also at high energy when |g|ħμ/k ≪ 1. For an attractive potential with g < −0.84 there is a bound state, and the low-energy cross section is far from Born.

Units ħ = m = 1. Double barrier: lengths in units of the well width b, energies in units of ħ²/(mb²), time in units of mb²/ħ. Yukawa: lengths in units of 1/μ, momenta in units of ħμ, cross sections in units of 1/μ².

Numerics: double barrier by transfer matrices; the packet is a sum over — exact scattering states, so any time can be reached directly, and the probabilities on each side come from the time-integrated current at the outer edges. Yukawa: Numerov integration of the radial equation for each partial wave, phase shifts by matching to Riccati–Bessel functions at r = 30/μ; the picture is the exact partial-wave sum.
